## Mission

Three front-ends share one brand. Audit the design system's completeness and, more importantly, its actual adoption — then define the visual language that makes Synaptic Go look like a product someone chose rather than settled for.

## Questions you must answer

1. Token inventory: colour, type scale, spacing, radius, elevation, motion — what exists in `app_theme.dart` and `tokens.ts`, and do the two agree? Produce the diff.
2. Adoption audit: how many hardcoded colours, magic spacing numbers and inline text styles remain across rider and captain? PR #12, #15, #18, #35 and #36 all attacked this — quantify what is left with `github__search_code`.
3. Dark mode: is it complete and correct on every screen, or does `GoTheme` get bypassed (PR #36 found nine screens reading the wrong source)? Which screens still break?
4. Component library completeness: what a ride-hailing app needs (map overlays, bottom sheets, offer cards, status chips, price steppers, rating widgets, avatars, badges, skeletons, toasts, empty and error states) versus what `flutter_shared/widgets` provides. List the gaps.
5. Duplication: components implemented separately in rider and captain that should be shared. `trip_chat_screen.dart` exists in both — how much is copy-paste?
6. Typography: is there one Arabic-capable family used consistently (IBM Plex Sans Arabic per the roadmap, but PR #18 mentions 'zero IBM Plex')? Resolve the contradiction. Assess the Arabic type scale, line height, and numeral style (Arabic-Indic vs Western digits — a real product decision for Egypt).
7. Brand: what does Synaptic Go actually look like? Evaluate the wordmark, the colour story, and the splash. Is the identity distinct from inDrive's green and Uber's black, or derivative? Propose a defensible direction with concrete values.
8. Map styling: the map is 70% of the pixels in this product and is currently default OSM tiles. Specify a branded map style (palette, POI density, night mode) and how to deliver it (MapTiler is already contemplated in the roadmap).
9. Iconography: consistent set, correct RTL mirroring, and optical alignment.
10. Elevation and glass: PR #26 and #35 mention glassy cards and a later 'lightening'. Is there a coherent surface system or accumulated fashion?
11. Density and hierarchy: does the primary action on each screen dominate? Assess the three most important screens (rider home, captain offer, trip in progress).
12. Admin↔mobile coherence: should they look like one family? Define the shared spine and the licensed divergences.

## Shared context (identical for every task)

**Synaptic Go** is an Egyptian ride-hailing platform, currently pre-production.
The product goal is a transport and delivery app with the feature depth of Uber
and the price-negotiation model of inDrive, hardened to full production quality.

**Stack.** Cloudflare Workers (Hono-style routing) + D1 (SQLite) + KV
(`SESSIONS`) + R2 (`FILES`) + four Durable Objects (`TripRoom`, `GeoCell`,
`CaptainInbox`, `OfferScheduler`) + Queues (`NOTIFICATIONS` with a DLQ) + two
cron triggers (scheduled-trip dispatch every minute, B2B invoices monthly).
Two Flutter apps (`apps/rider`, `apps/captain`) sharing `packages/flutter_shared`.
A React + Vite admin console (`apps/admin`). 19 D1 migrations. `APP_VERSION 0.4.0`.

**API surface.** `apps/api/src/routes/`: admin, auth, captain, companies,
devices, geocode, intercity, payments, promo, safety, search, trips, user,
wallet. `apps/api/src/lib/`: audit, cleanup, geocode, jwt, nearby, notifications,
paymob, pricing, routing, schemas, turnstile, types, utils.
`apps/api/src/middleware/`: auth, rateLimit.

**Existing planning docs** — read the ones relevant to your track and write *on
