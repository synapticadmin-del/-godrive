## Questions you must answer

1. Server authority: is the final fare computed server-side from server-known inputs, or can the client submit a price the server accepts? Trace every field the client sends that influences money.
2. The estimate: what inputs (distance, duration, vehicle type, surge, time of day) and from where — OSRM route or straight-line haversine? What is the error band, and what happens when OSRM is unreachable?
3. Bidding rules: is there a floor and ceiling on a rider's offer relative to the estimate? Can a rider offer 1 EGP for a 40 km trip and waste every captain's attention? Can a captain counter at 10,000 EGP?
4. Bid race: two captains accept the same trip in the same instant — what serialises it? Quote the code. What does the loser see?
5. Counter-offer loop: how many rounds, what expires them, and what stops a captain from re-bidding endlessly to spam the rider's screen?
6. Surge: `pricing_rules.surge_multiplier` exists — is it computed from live supply/demand, set manually in admin, or dormant? If dormant, design the computation: geographic cell, time window, smoothing, cap, and rider disclosure.
7. Price lock: once a rider accepts a captain's price, is it immutable? What can change it afterwards (waiting time, route change, extra stop, tolls) and is any of that implemented?
8. Promos: stacking rules, per-user caps, expiry, minimum fare, and whether the discount is charged to the platform or silently to the captain. The latter is a captain-trust catastrophe — verify which it is.
9. Cancellation fees and no-show charges: are they modelled at all? Who pays, after how long, and what stops abuse in both directions?
10. Waiting time, tolls, night rate, airport surcharge, intercity per-seat pricing — which exist, which are absent.
11. Vehicle types: how do `vehicle_types` map to price multipliers, and is the mapping enforced server-side at settlement?
12. Unit economics: build the model for a representative Cairo trip — gross fare, commission, PSP fee, infrastructure cost per trip, incentive cost — and state the contribution margin.

## Benchmark against

- inDrive: the canonical rider-offer/driver-counter model — offer bounds anchored to a recommended price, limited counter rounds, no surge but transparent demand hints.
- Uber: upfront pricing with server-side quote signing, surge as a multiplier with rider confirmation, dynamic pickup-point pricing.
- Careem: fare-lock with explicit waiting-time and toll line items on the receipt.
