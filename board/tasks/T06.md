## Questions you must answer

1. Geospatial indexing: how are captain positions bucketed into `GeoCell`? Cell size, neighbour search (PR #8 added adjacent-cell search — verify), and behaviour at cell boundaries and across city edges.
2. Candidate ranking: what orders the captain list — pure distance, ETA, rating, acceptance rate, idle time, vehicle type match? Straight-line or routed distance?
3. Offer rollout: PR #14 introduced batched rollout by proximity. What are the batch sizes and intervals, what is the total time to exhaust candidates, and what happens when nobody accepts?
4. Accept race: quote the exact code that prevents two captains from both winning a trip. Is it a conditional UPDATE, a DO lock, or optimistic and broken? What does the losing captain's UI show?
5. Stale position: how fresh must a captain's location be to be a candidate? What is the location update cadence from `captain_state.dart`, and what does battery-saving mode do to it (PR #23)?
6. Online-state integrity: PR #4 added an online guard. Can a captain receive offers while offline, backgrounded, or with the app killed? Is state server-authoritative?
7. Fairness and starvation: does the same nearby captain get every offer while others idle? Is there any round-robin, cooldown after rejection, or acceptance-rate weighting? What stops cherry-picking of high-value trips only?
8. Scheduled trips: the cron runs every minute (`wrangler.toml`). How far ahead does dispatch start, what happens if no captain is found, and is the rider told in time?
9. No-supply path: what does the rider experience when zero captains are online in their cell? Is there a queue, a widening radius, a notify-me-when-available, or a dead end?
10. DO lifecycle and cost: how many `GeoCell` instances exist at Cairo scale, what wakes them, and what does that cost per day? Is there hibernation?
11. Reassignment: when a captain cancels after accepting, does the trip re-enter dispatch automatically, and does the rider keep their position?
12. City scoping: `captain_city` filtering — how does an intercity or cross-city-boundary request behave?

## Benchmark against

- Uber: batched dispatch with ETA-based ranking, driver-side acceptance-rate consequences, and forward-dispatch (offering the next trip before the current one ends).
- inDrive: broadcast-to-many with rider choice — a fundamentally different model that this codebase partially implements; be explicit about which model the product is actually committing to.
- Careem: supply-positioning heatmaps to move captains toward demand before requests arrive.

## Shared context (identical for every task)

**Synaptic Go** is an Egyptian ride-hailing platform, currently pre-production.
The product goal is a transport and delivery app with the feature depth of Uber
and the price-negotiation model of inDrive, hardened to full production quality.
