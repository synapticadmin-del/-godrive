## Questions you must answer

1. Intercity: trace the full booking flow — route and schedule definition, seat inventory, hold/reserve, payment, QR verification, boarding, cancellation and refund (0010). What is complete, what is a stub, and what has no UI?
2. Seat-inventory concurrency: PR #13 mentions race protection on seat booking. Verify it — quote the SQL. Can the same seat be sold twice?
3. Intercity captain experience: how does a captain get assigned to a scheduled route, see their manifest, and verify passengers? Is any of this in the captain app?
4. Intercity pricing: per-seat, per-route, dynamic by occupancy? How does it interact with the bidding model — or does it bypass it entirely?
5. B2B: trace the corporate flow — company creation, employee invitation and binding, spend limits, vehicle policy, trip tagging, and the monthly invoice cron. Who is the admin for a company, and is there any company-facing interface at all, or is everything done by platform staff?
6. Invoice generation: what does the cron actually produce, where does it go, and is there a PDF? `scripts/generate_pdf.py` exists — is it connected to anything?
7. B2B gaps: cost centres, approval workflows, reporting exports, credit terms, VAT handling for Egyptian corporate invoicing. Which matter for a first corporate customer?
8. Admin blindness: neither vertical has an admin page. Specify both consoles (coordinate with T11) — what an operator needs to run intercity schedules and corporate accounts day to day.
9. Delivery/courier vertical: scope it properly. What changes in the data model (parcel, sender, recipient, proof of delivery, size/weight classes), in dispatch (different vehicle types, batching, multi-drop), in pricing, in the captain app (pickup confirmation, photo proof, recipient signature), and in risk (damaged or lost goods)? Estimate effort honestly.
10. Other verticals worth considering: motorcycle/tuk-tuk classes for Cairo traffic, rentals with driver, airport fixed-price transfers, and scheduled commuter subscriptions. Assess each for market fit and effort, and recommend a sequence.
11. Which vertical earns the next quarter of engineering time? Argue it with market and effort reasoning, not enthusiasm.

## Shared context (identical for every task)

**Synaptic Go** is an Egyptian ride-hailing platform, currently pre-production.
The product goal is a transport and delivery app with the feature depth of Uber
and the price-negotiation model of inDrive, hardened to full production quality.

**Stack.** Cloudflare Workers (Hono-style routing) + D1 (SQLite) + KV
(`SESSIONS`) + R2 (`FILES`) + four Durable Objects (`TripRoom`, `GeoCell`,
`CaptainInbox`, `OfferScheduler`) + Queues (`NOTIFICATIONS` with a DLQ) + two
cron triggers (scheduled-trip dispatch every minute, B2B invoices monthly).
Two Flutter apps (`apps/rider`, `apps/captain`) sharing `packages/flutter_shared`.
A React + Vite admin console (`apps/admin`). 19 D1 migrations. `APP_VERSION 0.4.0`.

**API surface.** `apps/api/src/routes/`: admin, auth, captain, companies,
devices, geocode, intercity, payments, promo, safety, search, trips, user,
wallet. `apps/api/src/lib/`: audit, cleanup, geocode, jwt, nearby, notifications,
paymob, pricing, routing, schemas, turnstile, types, utils.
`apps/api/src/middleware/`: auth, rateLimit.

**Existing planning docs** — read the ones relevant to your track and write *on
