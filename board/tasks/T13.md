## Mission

Motion is where this product can feel expensive at almost no infrastructure cost. Audit what animation exists, design the motion system that should exist, and use it to hide the latency the architecture cannot remove.

## Questions you must answer

1. Inventory every animation that currently exists, its duration and curve, and whether the values are tokens or magic numbers.
2. Splash: PR #39 and #41 added launch motion and there is a `splash.mp4`. Is a video the right mechanism (size, decode cost, cold-start delay on low-end Android) versus a vector or Rive animation? Measure the cold-start impact.
3. The moments that deserve choreography — name them and specify each: request sent, first bid arriving, bid accepted, captain assigned, captain arriving, trip start, trip complete, payment success, going online. For each: what the user is feeling, and the motion that acknowledges it.
4. Map marker movement: does the car marker jump between location updates or interpolate along the route? Specify the interpolation (tween duration matched to update interval, bearing rotation, easing) — this single change is the difference between amateur and professional.
5. Bottom sheets: are transitions native-feeling, gesture-driven, and interruptible? Do they respect the keyboard?
6. List insertion: bids arriving in `captain_bids_sheet.dart` — are they animated in, and is the list stable (no reordering jump under the user's thumb)?
7. Skeletons vs spinners: where is each used, and is the skeleton shaped like the content it replaces?
8. Perceived performance: identify the three longest waits in the product (estimate calculation, dispatch, payment) and design what fills them — progressive disclosure, optimistic UI, honest progress.
9. Haptics: where are they used, and where should they be (offer arrival, bid accepted, arrival confirmation)? Android and iOS parity.
10. Frame budget: any animation running on the platform thread, any jank on the map screen, any layout thrash. Assess with concrete Flutter mechanisms (`RepaintBoundary`, `AnimatedBuilder` scope, `const` constructors).
11. Accessibility: is `reduce motion` respected? (Coordinate with T15.) Every animation must have a still fallback.
12. Consistency: define the motion tokens — durations (instant/fast/normal/slow), curves (standard/decelerate/accelerate/spring), and the rule for which applies to what.

## Benchmark against

- Uber: marker interpolation and route-line drawing that make a 4-second GPS update feel continuous.
- inDrive: restrained, fast, functional — motion that never delays a driver's tap.
- General: Material 3 motion spec as a baseline, exceeded where the brand demands it.
