## Mission

Make the product usable by people with low vision, motor difficulty, or an old phone in bright sunlight — and by a driver who can only look for one second. Accessibility here is not compliance theatre; it overlaps almost entirely with usability under real Egyptian conditions.

## Questions you must answer

1. Contrast: compute the actual ratio of every foreground/background token pair in both themes against WCAG AA (4.5:1 text, 3:1 large text and UI). PR #? claimed WCAG AAA on admin — verify the claim numerically. List failures with measured values.
2. Touch targets: measure the interactive elements on the busiest screens against the 48×48 dp minimum. Name every offender.
3. Screen readers: is there any `Semantics` in the Flutter widgets, `semanticLabel` on icons, or ARIA in the admin? Test the critical path — can a blind rider request a trip? Be honest about the gap.
4. Dynamic type: does the UI survive the OS font scale at 130% and 200%? Which screens overflow or clip? This matters enormously for older users.
5. Colour as sole information carrier: status chips, map markers, trip states — is meaning conveyed by colour alone anywhere?
6. Focus management in the admin: keyboard navigation, visible focus rings, modal focus trapping (`RejectionReasonModal`, `QuickSearchModal`), and escape-to-close.
7. Motion sensitivity: is `MediaQuery.disableAnimations` / `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured? (Coordinate with T13.)
8. Glanceability for drivers: the offer card judged at a one-second glance — what is legible at arm's length in sunlight? Specify minimum sizes and contrast for the driving context specifically.
9. One-handed reach: are primary actions within thumb reach on a 6.7-inch device, or in the top corners?
10. Form accessibility: labels bound to inputs, error text associated with the field, and whether errors are announced.
11. Low-literacy support: iconography plus text, voice input for destination, and whether the flow can be completed by someone who reads slowly.
12. Assistive-technology testing plan: what to test, on what devices, and how often — a repeatable checklist, not a one-off audit.

## Benchmark against

- WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor; name the specific success criteria that fail.
- Uber and Careem both ship documented accessibility support including screen-reader-complete booking flows.
- Practical benchmark: can a 60-year-old with reading glasses and a 3-year-old Android phone book a ride without help?
